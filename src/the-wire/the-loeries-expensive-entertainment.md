- **Experience without distinctiveness wastes investment.**

- **Consumer insight must precede spectacle.**

- **Authenticity turns talent into equity.**

- **One powerful idea must travel.**

Brands are spending millions creating experiences consumers will never forget. But there's just one problem. They might forget the brand.

That was the tension sitting beneath The Game Has Changed: Building Brands in the Age of Experiences at Loeries 2026, where SAB's Vaughan Croeser was joined by Melanie Nicholson, Thapelo Mokoena and Fabiana Pereira to interrogate what experience-led brand building actually requires.

For CMOs, the question is brutally commercial:

> Are we building brand equity, or simply funding a good time?

## 1. Insight Before Spectacle

Great experiences don't start with an event. They start with human behaviour.

Melanie Nicholson's work with Corona Sunsets demonstrates the difference. Corona hasn't simply attached itself to music. It has built an experience around a deeper territory of nature, connection and being present.

As Nicholson has explained: "The festival is built around connection, connection to music, to nature, and to one another."

That distinction matters. Insight gives an experience strategic permission to exist. Spectacle merely gives people a reason to attend.

## 2. If Anyone Could Own It, Nobody Does

Actor Thapelo Mokoena's perspective introduces another crucial ingredient: talent cannot simply be rented reach.

The most powerful partnerships happen when creators genuinely want to inhabit the brand world. Stella Artois' experiences, built around ritual, craft and taking time over something worthwhile, create space for talent to participate rather than simply endorse.

The lesson: "Talent should never be the experience. They should make the brand experience more believable."

Because if your creator is more memorable than your brand, ask who is actually accumulating the equity.

## 3. Challenge the Category, Not the Consumer

Fabiana Pereira's Mozambique World Cup work offers perhaps the sharpest example of insight becoming innovation.

Mozambique had never qualified for the World Cup. Instead of treating that as exclusion, 2M turned it into freedom: a reversible jersey allowing supporters to switch allegiance when their adopted team lost. The campaign transformed a real fan behaviour into the creative idea itself.

The strategic lesson is powerful:

"Category conventions tell you what has always been done. Culture tells you what could happen next."

## 4. Make the Idea Travel

Vaughan Croeser's masterclass ultimately raises the biggest CMO challenge: scale. An experience reaching 500 people cannot only matter to 500 people.

Corona demonstrated this by extending Sunsets beyond the festival grounds through national television and streaming, turning a physical experience into a shared cultural moment.

The future isn't 20 disconnected activations. It is one powerful idea, multiplied across touchpoints without diluting its meaning.

Because reach without memory is noise. Experience without attribution is entertainment.

And experience without distinctiveness? That is simply very expensive entertainment.
