- **Culture is earned, never owned.**

- **50% of marketers prioritise authenticity.**

- **Co-creation beats cultural cosplay, always.**

- **Backlash costs trust, sales and leadership.**

Culture is no longer a channel. It's the price of entry. In 2026, 50% of marketers say authentic, unpolished content will be a major trend, while social communities, not paid reach, drive brand discovery.

The lesson? Brands don't own culture. They earn participation in it.

## The Big DO: Co-create, Don't Cosplay

**Local - Citiq x Phiri Movers FC:** Instead of "township-inspired" advertising, Citiq funded Soweto club Phiri Movers FC with bespoke kits from streetwear label Kasi Flavour - turning sponsorship into a genuine cultural drop, then scaling it through AFCON media.

**International - Tommy Hilfiger:** By joining TikTok's "airball" trend without forcing product messaging, the brand earned more than 2 million likes and praise in the comments.

Do this:

- Cede creative control to local creators and communities.
- Build with culture, not merely "for" it.
- Let participation, not a tagline, carry the idea.

## The Big DON'T: Borrow Sacred Stories for Sales

**Local - The Glenlivet:** Its Women's Month ad tied the 1956 Women's March and struggle icons to an 18-year-old whisky. The backlash was immediate; the campaign was pulled, apologies followed, and affected families called it "unauthorised corporate appropriation of a national heritage."

**International - Starbucks Korea:** A 2026 campaign perceived to reference the 1980 Gwangju Uprising triggered a "very significant" sales decline and the dismissal of Starbucks Korea's head.

Don't do this:

- Treat trauma, liberation history, or identity as aesthetic.
- Approve culturally sensitive work without community consultation.
- Confuse attention with trust - deletion doesn't undo the damage.

## The 2026 CMO Test

Before launch, ask:

> Would the community recognise this as a contribution, or an extraction?

Culture rewards brands that show up with humility, resources, and shared authorship. It punishes those that arrive with a camera crew and a sales target.
