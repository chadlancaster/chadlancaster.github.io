- **27,000+ conversations** later, the community proved people crave participation, not passive ads.

- **Communities decide what survives**. Discord rewards emergent culture, not polished control.

- **What if your brand became remixable?** The question separating campaigns from culture infrastructure.

The Marketing Industry has always obsessed over control. Protect the logo. Police the message. Own the narrative. Guard the brand guidelines, like sacred scripture.

Then an entire generation arrived and ignored the rules, ushered in a new form of culture marketing with new vibes and operating principles. The youth today don't experience brands the way previous generations did. [They sample them. Meme them. Mash them together. They borrow, remix and reinterpret culture](https://www.perplexity.ai/search/7cb9a900-9389-482c-8b32-aae8aa0a4445) at internet speed, turning products into identities and identities into communities overnight.

The uncomfortable truth? Your brand is no longer defined by what you create. It's defined by what people create with it.

That's the **Remix Economy**. And it's about to reshape marketing.

**From vibes to signals to more than a brand strategy**

We've already seen the signals. Fashion aesthetics like Y2K, Gorpcore and Cottagecore weren't created by brands. They were assembled by communities pulling inspiration from thousands of cultural fragments. Music is no different. Samples become hits. Memes become movements. Creator edits often outperform the original campaign they were inspired by.

Culture no longer moves in straight lines.

It mutates.

The brands growing fastest are learning to mutate with it.

One of the most fascinating examples comes from an unlikely place.

Fast-food chain Jack in the Box didn't use Discord as another social media channel. It treated it as cultural infrastructure. During its [Comic-Con "Jack's Late Night Discord" experience,](https://www.marketingdive.com/news/why-jack-in-the-box-hosted-a-comic-con-afterparty-on-discord/604357/) the brand hosted a virtual Aquabats concert, opened fan-led Marvel and DC spaces and even auctioned NFT artwork created with community participation.

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=o_b5OO_w2_Y)

The results were extraordinary.

More than [**5,500 members joined within 24 hours**](https://www.ypulse.com/newsfeed/2021/08/06/jack-in-the-box-and-more-brands-are-tapping-discord-to-reach-young-audiences/), over **five times Discord's benchmark for branded communities**. By the end of the weekend, membership had reached **7,664 people**, generating **more than 27,000 conversations**.

Those numbers matter. But one metric matters even more. The community stayed. Most campaigns create impressions. This created a place people wanted to keep building. That's a fundamentally different role for a brand.

Research increasingly describes brands as **systems of meaning**, shaping trust, identity and belonging in an increasingly fragmented world. At the same time, trust has become one of the most valuable commercial assets a company can own, while culture continues splintering into thousands of micro-communities with their own symbols, language and rules. Broadcasting doesn't work particularly well inside fragmented culture. Participation does.

As Jesse Nicely, VP and Group Strategy Director at Cashmere Agency, observed, Discord's speed makes it fertile ground for emergent culture because communities, not marketers, determine what survives.

That's the future. Not user-generated content. User-generated culture. There's an enormous difference. The winners won't simply invite consumers into campaigns. They'll give them something worth extending. Imagine opening parts of your brand instead of locking everything down.

- A sonic identity creators can sample.

- Visual assets designed for remixing.

- Creator toolkits instead of campaign toolkits.

- Limited licensing frameworks that encourage experimentation instead of preventing it.

In other words, stop treating your brand like a museum. Start treating it like an open-world game. The brands dominating the next decade won't produce the most culture. They'll enable the most culture.

The KPI won't be reach. It won't even be engagement. It will be **remixability**:

- How many creators borrowed from you?

- How many communities adapted you?

- How many cultural moments happened because your brand became useful to someone else's story?

That's no longer marketing. That's infrastructure. And infrastructure always outlasts advertising.

**Before approving your next campaign, ask one question: If people started remixing our brand tomorrow... would we fight them, or would we have designed for it?**
