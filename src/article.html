The Afrobeats Atlas: where the genre is going next.

Mapping the genre's next five movements - from Accra to São Paulo to Atlanta. A field report on what's shaping global music's most-streamed sound, who's making it, and what brands need to understand before they show up.

Words Robert Greeff
Published 6 June 2026
Reading time 6 min
X In Link

Five years ago, Afrobeats was a genre the global music industry was still learning to spell. Today it headlines stadiums from London to Los Angeles, soundtracks fashion campaigns and shapes the rhythm of pop itself. But the artists and producers building it are already looking past the label - toward something bigger, stranger and more regional than a single word can hold.

Walk into a studio in Accra, Lagos or Johannesburg right now and you'll hear it: a restlessness with the formula that made the sound famous. The 3am log-drum sessions, the diaspora collaborations, the tempo experiments - they all point the same way. The next chapter of African music won't be a genre. It'll be a map.

The sound is fragmenting - on purpose

For a decade, "Afrobeats" worked as a passport. It got artists onto festival bills, into brand briefs and up the streaming charts. But the term always flattened more than it described, and the people making the music know it best.

What's emerging instead is a constellation of regional scenes, each with its own tempo, its own visual language and its own idea of what "global" means. Amapiano's patient, hypnotic build is a different proposition to the melodic sprint of a Lagos street-pop record - and audiences are increasingly fluent in the difference.

The next decade of African music won't be one sound. It'll be ten scenes - each with its own capital, its own tempo and its own idea of what global means. - Kwabena Osei, A&R, Accra

Where the movement is heading

Our editorial desk has been tracking the signals - release patterns, playlist migration, festival routing and the quiet money moving into local scenes. Five capitals keep surfacing:

  • Accra - a melodic, guitar-forward strain of highlife-pop that's winning sync deals faster than anywhere on the continent.
  • Johannesburg - amapiano's second wind, now exporting producers as much as tracks.
  • São Paulo - the Afro-Brazilian bridge, where baile funk and Afrobeats are quietly merging.
  • Atlanta - the diaspora studio hub turning African rhythm into American radio.
  • Paris - the fashion-and-francophone axis routing West African talent into European culture.

What it means for brands

The temptation is to treat "Afrobeats" as a single audience you can buy into. That window has closed. The brands winning here are the ones showing up scene by scene - backing a specific movement early, in a way that feels native to it, rather than sponsoring the word.

That takes cultural fluency, not just a media plan. It means knowing the difference between a moment and a movement, and being willing to build the long relationship rather than rent the trend. The map is being drawn right now. The question for every brand is whether it wants to be on it.
